## 1. Project Overview

This project presents the schematic design and two-layer PCB layout of a Smart Automatic Night Light Controller using eSim.

The circuit is designed to detect changes in ambient light intensity and automatically control an LED. It uses a Light Dependent Resistor (LDR) as the sensing element, an LM393 comparator for threshold detection, and a 2N7000 N-channel MOSFET for LED switching.

A 10 kΩ potentiometer provides an adjustable reference voltage, allowing the switching threshold to be changed according to the required lighting conditions.

The project was developed as part of Screening Task 7 for the FOSSEE Winter Internship 2026.

The main focus was to complete the PCB design workflow, from schematic capture and footprint assignment to routing, design-rule verification, and 3D visualization.

## 3. Working Principle

The proposed circuit operates by comparing a light-dependent voltage with an adjustable reference voltage.

### 3.1 Light Detection

The light-sensing section consists of an LDR (R1) and a fixed 10 kΩ resistor (R2).

These components form a voltage divider connected between the 5V supply and ground.

The output voltage of this divider depends on the resistance of the LDR.

The sensor voltage is given by:

**V_sensor = 5 × R_LDR / (10,000 + R_LDR)**

where R_LDR represents the instantaneous resistance of the LDR in ohms.

When ambient light intensity increases, the LDR resistance decreases, causing the sensor voltage to fall.

When the surroundings become darker, the LDR resistance increases, causing the sensor voltage to rise.

### 3.2 Voltage Comparison

The LM393 comparator compares the sensor voltage with the reference voltage generated by the 10 kΩ potentiometer (RV1).

The connections are:

- Non-inverting input (+): LDR voltage divider output.
- Inverting input (−): Adjustable potentiometer output.
- Comparator output: MOSFET gate-control circuit.

The LM393 has an open-collector output, so a 10 kΩ pull-up resistor (R4) is connected to the 5V supply.

When the sensor voltage exceeds the reference voltage, the comparator output transistor is switched off, allowing the output voltage to rise through R4.

When the sensor voltage falls below the reference, the comparator pulls its output low.

### 3.3 LED Switching

A 2N7000 N-channel MOSFET acts as the switching device.

Its gate is connected to the LM393 output. The drain is connected to the LED cathode, while the source is connected to ground.

A 330 Ω resistor (R3) limits the LED current.

The intended switching behavior is:

| Lighting condition | Sensor voltage | Comparator output | LED |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bright surroundings | Below reference | LOW | OFF |
| Dark surroundings | Above reference | HIGH through pull-up | ON |

This table describes the intended operation based on the circuit topology. Functional simulation and physical testing have not yet been performed.

## 5. Schematic Design

The circuit schematic was created using the KiCad Schematic Editor integrated with eSim.

The design was divided into four functional sections:

**Power Supply:** A two-pin connector supplies 5V and GND to the circuit. A 100 nF ceramic capacitor is connected across the supply for decoupling.

**Sensor Circuit:** The LDR and a 10 kΩ fixed resistor form a voltage divider that produces a light-dependent signal.

**Comparator Circuit:** The LM393 compares the sensor voltage against the adjustable reference voltage. A pull-up resistor provides the HIGH output state.

**Output Circuit:** The 2N7000 MOSFET controls current through the LED and its series resistor.

After completing the schematic connections, the Electrical Rules Checker was used to identify and resolve electrical-rule violations.

The final ERC run reported zero errors and zero warnings.

## 6. PCB Design and Layout

The PCB was designed using KiCad 9.0.3 through the eSim 2.6 workflow.

### 6.1 Footprint Assignment

Through-hole footprints were selected for the components.

The major footprints include:

| Component | Footprint |
|---|---|
| LM393 | DIP-8, 7.62 mm width |
| Fixed resistors | DIN0207 axial resistor |
| Ceramic capacitor | 5 mm disc capacitor |
| LED | 5 mm through-hole LED |
| 2N7000 | TO-92 Inline |
| LDR | Vertical LDR footprint |
| Potentiometer | Bourns 3296W Vertical |
| Power connector | Phoenix 2-pin, 5 mm pitch |

The footprint assignments were checked against the schematic pin numbers. Physical compatibility with the exact purchased parts should be confirmed before fabrication.

### 6.2 Component Placement

The components were arranged according to their functions and electrical connections.

The power connector was positioned toward one side of the PCB, followed by the sensor network and comparator.

The LED and MOSFET were placed toward the output side, while the potentiometer was positioned where its adjustment screw could be accessed.

The decoupling capacitor was placed near the LM393.

The layout was organized to reduce unnecessary routing distances while keeping sufficient spacing between component footprints.

### 6.3 PCB Routing

The PCB uses two copper layers:

- **F.Cu:** Front copper layer.
- **B.Cu:** Back copper layer.

A nominal signal track width of 0.30 mm was configured during layout.

Most signal and supply connections were routed on the front copper layer.

The back copper layer was used where necessary to complete connections that could not be routed conveniently on the front.

The routing was performed using KiCad's interactive routing tools, which helped maintain the configured electrical clearances.

### 6.4 Ground Plane

A copper-filled zone connected to the GND net was added on the back copper layer.

The ground plane provides a common ground connection for the circuit components and reduces the need for individual ground traces.

Thermal relief connections were used for through-hole pads where applicable.

The copper zone was refilled before running the final PCB verification.

## 9. Instructions to Open the Project

### Software Requirements

- Windows 11 or another operating system supported by the required eSim release.
- eSim 2.6.
- KiCad 9.x, preferably the version bundled with eSim 2.6.
- Standard KiCad symbol and footprint libraries.

### Opening the Project

1. Download the project folder from the GitHub repository.
2. Extract the project files if necessary.
3. Install and open eSim 2.6.
4. Open the existing project using the supplied project files.
5. If opening through KiCad directly, use the KiCad 9 Project Manager and select `Task7_Smart_Night_Light.kicad_pro`.
6. Open the Schematic Editor to inspect the circuit.
7. Open the PCB Editor to inspect the layout, tracks, and copper ground zone.

Avoid opening and saving the project using older KiCad versions without checking compatibility.

### Running Verification

**Schematic verification:**

1. Open the schematic.
2. Select `Inspect → Electrical Rules Checker`.
3. Click `Run ERC`.
4. Review the results.

**PCB verification:**

1. Open the PCB Editor.
2. Select `Inspect → Design Rules Checker`.
3. Enable `Refill all zones before performing DRC`.
4. Enable `Test for parity between PCB and schematic`.
5. Click `Run DRC`.
6. Review the violation, unconnected-item, and schematic-parity results.

### Viewing the PCB in 3D

1. Open the PCB Editor.
2. Select `View → 3D Viewer`.
3. Rotate the PCB to inspect the component arrangement.

If component bodies are missing, install the required KiCad 3D models and configure `KICAD9_3DMODEL_DIR` to point to the directory containing the `.3dshapes` folders.

The project should not require changes to its electrical connections merely to display the 3D models.

## 10. Current Project Status and Limitations

The schematic and PCB design have been completed, and the enabled ERC and DRC checks have been run successfully.

The PCB has also been inspected through KiCad's 3D Viewer.

However, the following work remains outside the scope of the completed verification:

- Functional circuit simulation has not been performed.
- Physical PCB fabrication and assembly have not been performed.
- Hardware testing under different lighting conditions has not been performed.
- Exact component dimensions and manufacturer-specific pinouts require final verification before fabrication.
- Switching stability around the threshold should be evaluated during future testing.

The circuit does not currently include a dedicated hysteresis network. Under slowly changing light conditions, switching near the threshold may be sensitive to noise.

## 11. Possible Future Improvements

Further development could include comparator hysteresis to prevent LED flickering near the switching threshold, reverse-polarity protection at the power input, and improvements to the PCB's mechanical layout.

After fabrication, the circuit could also be tested under different ambient light conditions to measure the switching threshold and evaluate the operation of the MOSFET output stage.
